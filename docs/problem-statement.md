# Problem Statement

## Analog Front-End for Biomedical Sensors

Biomedical sensors generate very small electrical signals that contain important physiological information. Neural signals such as electroencephalography (EEG), local field potentials (LFP), and extracellular action potentials (EAP) are typically low-amplitude signals and are highly susceptible to noise, interference, and unwanted frequency components.

The objective of this project is to design a **low-noise, low-power Analog Front-End (AFE)** for implantable or wearable biomedical neural-signal acquisition systems.

The proposed AFE will amplify and condition the weak signal generated by the biomedical sensor while maintaining high input impedance, low input-referred noise, appropriate bandwidth, and very low power consumption.

The scope of this project is limited to the **analog signal-conditioning chain up to the filtering stage**. ADC and digital signal-processing circuits are outside the scope of this design.

### Proposed Signal Chain

**Biomedical Sensor → Low-Noise Amplifier (LNA) → Programmable Gain Stage → Analog Filters → Conditioned Analog Output**

### Key Design Requirements

* Design a low-noise front-end suitable for weak biomedical neural signals.
* Provide sufficient gain to amplify low-amplitude sensor signals.
* Maintain high input impedance to minimize loading of the biomedical sensor.
* Provide appropriate frequency response for the selected neural-signal application.
* Achieve low input-referred noise.
* Minimize power consumption, targeting **less than 5 µW per channel**.
* Include a programmable or configurable gain stage.
* Implement appropriate analog filtering to suppress unwanted frequency components and interference.
* Maintain stable operation at a low supply voltage suitable for implantable or wearable applications.
* Evaluate the complete analog signal-conditioning chain through transistor-level simulations.
* Characterize gain, bandwidth, noise, power consumption, input impedance, and filter response.

### Scope of the Design

The project will focus on the design and optimization of the following analog blocks:

1. **Low-Noise Amplifier (LNA)**
2. **Programmable Gain Stage**
3. **Analog Filters**

The ADC, digital signal-processing circuitry, and subsequent digital data-processing stages are **outside the scope of this project**.

### Expected Outcome

The final outcome will be a transistor-level **low-noise, low-power biomedical Analog Front-End** capable of amplifying and filtering weak neural signals while achieving an appropriate trade-off between **gain, noise, bandwidth, input impedance, and power consumption**.

The complete analog chain will be validated using simulations, and its performance will be compared against the defined design targets and relevant published biomedical AFE architectures.
